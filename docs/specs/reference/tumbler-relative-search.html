    Relative

    What is happening

    The search vector becomes one of the three axes on screen, the vertical one. A point's height is its vector measured along the search direction. These vectors all have length one, so height is exactly the cosine similarity, and the scale on the axis can be read like a ruler. The other two axes are not chosen by the search. They tour the strongest directions of whatever remains after the search direction is removed, so the view still tumbles.

    What to watch for

    The search sits at the top of its own axis and everything else hangs below it. While the view turns, points swing sideways and never change height. A stem joins each result to the height it is read at. With a second search, its direction is drawn as a second ruler from the origin: the plane holding both is shown exactly, and only the depth still tumbles.

    The origin

    Centring on the search and centring on the true origin (the all-zero vector) give the same picture, moved up or down. Tick Show the origin to zoom out and see it: the origin at the foot of the axis, the search one unit above, and the whole set crowded into a narrow band between 0.5 and 0.85.

    With the reranker

    A reranker is a second model that reads the search text against each candidate's text and scores the pair. It does not move anything: height stays the embedding's cosine. It changes which points are named and in what order, so its picks are numbered. A numbered point that sits below un-named ones is a place where the two models disagree, and the dashed line marks how far down the candidates reached.

    What it cannot show

    Sideways position is still a shadow. Two points at the same height and side by side on screen can be far apart in directions the view does not hold. The figure in the status line says how much of the remaining spread the tumbling axes can show at all.

    Plain

    What is happening

    This is Tumble as it ships. The middle of the screen is the average of all the vectors, and the three axes tour the eight directions along which the set spreads most. A search lights its results and marks the query, and nothing is measured from it.

    What to watch for

    Tick Show the origin. The origin lands near the middle of the cloud, yet it is far from every point: the average vector is most of a unit away from it, in a direction the tour never shows. Distances from the middle of this view are distances from the average, not similarities.
