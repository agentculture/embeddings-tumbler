Tumble

What is happening

A rotating tesseract is a 4-D shape seen through a 3-D window that keeps turning. This is the same trick with 384 dimensions. The three axes you see are a small frame that drifts smoothly through the whole space and never returns to the same view.

What to watch for

Regions slide through each other and separate again. The ring of months opens into a circle, then flattens to a line. Filaments between regions swing like bridges. None of the points move. Only the viewpoint does.

Lattice

What is happening

A thousand of the points are wired to their three nearest neighbours, measured in all 384 dimensions, so the wires stay true even when the picture misleads. The view changes one axis at a time: a visible axis is traded for a hidden direction, the way a tesseract turns inside out.

What to watch for

A wire flares when the view shows it close to its true length and fades when it points off into dimensions you cannot see. A long wire joins two real neighbours that this view happens to pull apart. Two clumps that overlap with no wires between them are not actually close.

Wander

What is happening

Instead of watching from outside, the camera travels. It hops from one named star to a near neighbour, and at every stop the three axes are fitted again to that neighbourhood, so each region unfolds along its own strongest directions. Every few hops it leaps somewhere far away.

What to watch for

Brightness is true distance. Bright points are close in all 384 dimensions. The dim dust is far away, however near it looks on screen. Spokes mark the nearest named neighbours of the current stop, and the ring of months turns face-on when the camera arrives there.

The data is synthetic: nine topic regions, the filaments and bridges between them, a ring of months and an arc of temperature words, generated to behave like the output of a small sentence-embedding model and normalised to unit length. 290 points carry words; the rest stand in for unnamed passages. Real model output is noisier and less tidy. Because there is no model here, a typed query is placed from the named stars it mentions; the reranker is a toy that favours the topic most results share. Distances are measured in all 384 dimensions, so a result can look far away in the picture and still be near. The view never stops moving during a search. A new search swings the view, for a few seconds, toward the one whose first axes join the centres of the searches: clusters that still touch from there are close in the space, not only in the picture. Then the view tumbles on with the filter kept. Face the searches swings it back.
